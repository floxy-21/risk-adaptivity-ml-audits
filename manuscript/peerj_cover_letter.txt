Dear Academic Editor,

Please consider "Risk and Adaptivity in Certifying Machine-Learning Model
Comparisons" as a Research Article in PeerJ Computer Science.

The paper addresses an algorithmic question in machine-learning evaluation:
when verification targets are incomplete, when does observing one measurement
before choosing another improve the guarantee for a model-comparison interval?
It treats the unknown record as fixed and evaluates acquisition randomness,
making the information available to the evaluator explicit.

The main result characterizes the nondecreasing penalties of terminal sharp
interval width for which randomized batch and adaptive querying have equal
minimax risk for every compact convex uncertainty set and finite linear-query
dictionary. The proof includes the nonconcave-penalty converse using ordinary
coordinate queries. Exact two-query algorithms and checkable adverse-completion
certificates translate the distinction into a computable acquisition decision.
The manuscript explicitly credits established minimax, optimal-recovery,
robust-optimization and sparse-policy results and distinguishes their objectives.

Evaluation on two public sources identifies 290 of 4,914 and 29 of 1,674
forecast-audit tasks where the adaptive precision guarantee is one and the
optimal randomized-batch worst-case success is one half. The much smaller
overall recorded improvements are reported alongside those guarantees. A
declared measurement-error sensitivity requires two readings for 22 external
cases and three for seven. A measured public API favors bulk retrieval, and
the supplied selector consequently chooses bulk when that interface is
admissible. The application claims concern assurance-compatible acquisition
under explicit constraints; they do not assert general operational superiority
of adaptive retrieval or a measured reduction in financial cost.

The work fits algorithms and analysis of algorithms, artificial intelligence,
and data mining and machine learning through the verification and acquisition
problem, rather than a new predictive architecture. The self-contained editable
source and research_supplement.zip provide the proof, code, frozen inputs,
per-case certificates, source provenance and one entry point for offline
reproduction. The acquisition wrapper binds a saved comparison to its meter,
timestamps, coefficients, units and declared interface before dispatch; separate
checks verify those bindings, reconstruct retained counter endpoints, and
reject malformed requests and readouts.
This software integration selects among supplied certificates. Separate
regeneration and refitting routes are identified.
