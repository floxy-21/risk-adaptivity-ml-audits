The paper studies when feedback helps certify machine-learning comparisons under a fixed measurement budget. Its new central boundary uses only two affine targets and two unit-cost coordinate queries. For every integer m >= 3, adaptation guarantees the required precision while the optimal randomized fixed batch has worst-case success exactly 1/m. Each target separately admits a fixed two-query guarantee. The separation also holds for all three paired squared-loss contrasts of three constructed nonnegative forecast vectors. The main text supplies the feasible set, policies and matching bounds against every admissible randomized batch.

A scalar risk classification and a separate sparse-policy bound provide the foundation. The latter gives the one-half batch floor for a single target. The new construction keeps the budget and number of targets fixed while its measurement dictionary grows; its normalized margin shrinks, and three fixed queries suffice. We distinguish this exact assurance boundary from established vector recovery, minimax and sparse-policy results. The earlier 1/2-versus-2/3 joint mean-width separation and worked real-data certificate remain as complementary results.

The application compares four frozen forecasting models using two cumulative-meter readings shared across all six model pairs.
