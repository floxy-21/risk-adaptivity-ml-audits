SOURCE DATA AND ATTRIBUTION

1. UCI Individual Household Electric Power Consumption
   https://doi.org/10.24432/C58K54
   https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/235/individual+household+electric+power+consumption
   License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0), as listed by UCI.
   Minute-average global active power is kW. Hourly kWh equals the sum of all
   sixty finite minute readings divided by 60. One French household is one case;
   many days from that household do not constitute independently sampled homes.

2. UCI ElectricityLoadDiagrams20112014
   https://doi.org/10.24432/C58C86
   https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/321/electricityloaddiagrams20112014
   License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0), as listed by UCI.
   Original readings are quarter-hour kW. End-of-interval timestamps are shifted
   back 15 minutes before hourly aggregation; summing four readings and dividing
   by four yields hourly kWh. The provider uses a fixed Portuguese local-time
   grid with documented daylight-saving anomalies. Eligibility and exclusions
   follow the protocol and study code; meter selection uses training data only.

3. BLEM / Discovergy cumulative meter observations
   Michael Kostmann and Wolfgang K. Härdle (2019), Forecasting in Blockchain-Based
   Local Energy Markets, Energies 12, 2718. https://doi.org/10.3390/en12142718
   Original source: https://github.com/QuantLet/BLEM
   Pinned commit: 756b2c1265f964b70f72625a95d98413cdfb78bd
   Consumer IDs 00000001, 00000002 and 00000003 were selected lexicographically,
   before their forecast results were examined. The released time column is
   Unix milliseconds. The energy column is an integer cumulative import register;
   the original authors' FUN_getData.R converts counter increments by 1e-10 kWh.
   Our preparation subtracts integers before conversion and uses exact UTC hour
   endpoints. Each hour must contain twenty consecutive, nonnegative 3-minute
   increments. All three records satisfy this condition throughout the released
   year. No interpolation, tolerance matching, or empirical capacity cap is used.
   The generated data_blem_hourly_endpoints.csv retains both raw integer counters
   and actual timestamp endpoints; all matching lags are zero.
   The source article is CC BY 4.0, but a separate dataset license was not found.
   Public repository accessibility is not treated as permission to relicense the
   raw data. Raw BLEM source files and the local study_predictions.csv containing
   derived hourly verification targets are excluded from this submission archive.
   Source links, attribution, and download/reconstruction code are supplied.

WHAT THE EXPERIMENT CAN AND CANNOT ESTABLISH

The method evaluates fixed forecasts when some verification targets are unavailable
but surviving block totals restrict their feasible values. It does not train a new
forecast architecture or claim improved forecasting accuracy. Synthetic masks are
applied only after all forecasts have been generated.

For both UCI sources, surviving aggregate totals are derived from complete retained
truth before masking: aggregate survival is simulated. BLEM supplies actual
cumulative registers, whose endpoint differences yield exact interval energies,
but missing-target outages are still simulated. No field deployment, independently
surviving telemetry channel, or real-world outage probability has been measured.

The main feasibility sets impose nonnegative targets and exact/uncertain aggregate
totals, with no capacity estimated from observed maxima. Deterministic certificates
are conditional on these measurement assumptions. They concern the finite evaluated
cases and do not provide population-level significance or confidence in future data.
The highest-demand removal pattern is an oracle stress test, not a claimed empirical
outage mechanism. Three BLEM consumers form an external case series. Whole-meter
bootstrap intervals are descriptive, especially unstable with only three meters;
days, model pairs, overlapping masks and repeated seeds are not independent homes.

INDEPENDENTLY INTERVAL-BOUNDED TOTALS

interval_total_recovery.py is a separate batch solver for blocks with finite
0 <= lower <= sum(unknown targets) <= upper, nonnegative uncapped targets,
exact recovered answers, disjoint coordinate indices and equal query cost.
Its IntervalTotalBlock and RecoverySolution interfaces are documented in source.
It allows querying all coordinates, since a final coordinate is not generally
determined by an uncertain total; constant coefficients can retain uncertainty.
It does not alter the exact-total empirical run or claim field benefits.

Independent synthetic verification, with SciPy/NumPy installed:
  python outputs/study_interval_total_recovery.py

The generated JSON records LP extrema, exhaustive query enumeration, conditional
query-answer cases, exact-total reductions, invalid inputs and source hashes.
work/compose_interval_sections.py inserts the verified theory and check counts
into the same final manuscript. Floating-point outputs are not outward-rounded
machine certificates. Correlated totals and uncertain query answers require
additional modeling and are outside this solver.
