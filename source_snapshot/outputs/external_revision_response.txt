1. External source and actual new fits
The initial REFIT raw-source quality screen admitted no complete days under
the frozen complete-support rule. Its failure and source-only amendments

acquisition outcome. Twenty households were selected using training data;

Temporal totals, outages and archive cutoffs are constructed benchmark
conditions, not production incidents or independently surviving registers.

2. Consistent early and later archives
New causal forecasts use D-2, D-3 and D-8 inputs and a noon D-1 issue rule.
Every issued record available by each cutoff is included. No early issued
predictor depends on the missing day D values. The later persistence rows
reveal all D targets without a paid request. Copy values come from saved
forecast rows with source provenance, not from hidden reference lookups.
The benchmark clock is explicit; actual production arrival times and
physical UTC interpretation of source labels are not claimed.

3. Executable acquisition and measured decision cost
All five policies use the same observations/totals/certificate, charge the
entire selected batch, skip certified cases and stop within six requests.
The Hara comparator implements an explicitly labeled regression acquisition
adaptation using a pre-test surrogate. Requests remain separate per pair.
The exact routine optimizes each current worst-width batch; neither its
complete stopping sequence nor the shared cost of selecting all models is
claimed optimal. Both all-case and initially-uncertified coverage curves,
mean/median requests, unresolved counts and planning overhead are included.

Every policy has B80=4 on the test, also for learned-only comparisons.
Exact mean requested records are 2.4515, versus 2.4846 for greedy among
initially uncertified cases: 1.33% fewer on this secondary endpoint.
Exact conditional coverage is higher at two requests and slightly lower at
four; neither curve dominates. All comparisons are certified at the cap.
The later archive yields 100% strict certification with zero paid requests.
The improvement target is not attained and was not replaced after outcomes.

4. Checks and transparent logging correction
An independently written same-study checker replayed all 44,442 base cases,
266,652 policy/regime rows and 129,718 acquired batches.
Both periods have zero detected incorrect certificates, enclosure failures,
chronology violations, duplicate paid queries and cap violations.
Source features, complete case grids, all summaries and B80 were checked.
This is not an external investigator's replication or an independent
implementation of the learning algorithms.

The first replay found mutable aliasing in intermediate logged observation
arrays. The executable logger now copies snapshots. Original compressed
traces, original run metadata and executed source remain available.
Corrected snapshots use only recorded before states, batch answers and
aggregate deductions. Every original/repaired pair was compared: only
the after field changed, and each old discrepancy was exactly the next
batch's reveal mutation. Query selection, bounds, costs, decisions and all
numerical summary files are unchanged. A multistage regression fixture
passes for all five policies. This repair is not described as a new run.

Assessment
The finite methodological, implementation and reporting changes are done.
The evidence is stronger and more transparent, but it does not demonstrate
the requested large acquisition-cost advantage over greedy. The paper's
strongest contribution remains exact aggregate-constrained optimization and
certified comparison. This revision supports a methods-oriented submission
argument, not a high-probability applied-superiority or acceptance claim.
No journal submission has been made by this revision workflow.
