This assessment reads completed evaluator summaries. At the time of this
review, independent checks of the repaired traces were still running. It

The predeclared primary practical advantage was not obtained: every policy
has B80=4 on the test, both for all pairs and learned-model-only pairs. The

15% exact-versus-better-greedy/Hara criterion is therefore false, with a
zero relative B80 reduction. This belongs in the abstract, introduction,
results, discussion and conclusion; it cannot be replaced after the fact
with a favorable comparison against Hara or uniform alone.

Secondary test evidence is more differentiated. Among initially uncertified
comparisons, exact mean requests are 2.45151855 versus greedy 2.48461377:
0.03309521 fewer requests, or 1.33% relative. At budget2 their certification
rates are 77.6458% versus 75.9746%, while at budget 4 greedy is slightly higher:
99.5532% versus exact 99.5286%. Thus neither recorded curve dominates the
other. Exact has larger mean-cost differences from Hara and uniform, but
those do not override the stronger greedy comparator or the primary null
result. The generator now reports the signed secondary comparison without
assuming favorable outcomes.

Breadth is limited for the threshold metric: exact B80 is strictly smaller
than the better greedy/Hara value in 3/19 households and 1/6 pairs; there are
zero learned-only pair improvements. The improving pair is persistence
versus MLP. Pair-specific results must stay visible; they do not establish
general superiority in learned-model selection.

All-case early certification starts at 56.1237%. At the later constructed
archive, every eligible test comparison is certified without paid requests.
This is a valid controlled benchmark consequence of the two-day persistence
schedule, not a measured service-level deadline or retrieval saving. Full
coverage at the six-record cap is also partly structural: the imposed gap
contains six targets and aggregate deductions make full disclosure available
within that limit. It should not be promoted as distinctive policy efficacy.

The result is useful negative evidence about converting batch-width
optimality into sequential decision-cost savings. The formal exact
optimization and all-completions certificate remain the central contribution.
The new experiment does not substantiate the user's desired large applied
advantage over a strong elementary baseline, even if all integrity checks
pass. No effect-size or acceptance-probability claim should be manufactured.
