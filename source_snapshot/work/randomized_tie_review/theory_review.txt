INDEPENDENT MATHEMATICAL REVIEW OF RANDOMIZED MINIMAX TIE REFINEMENT

Scope: an exact-total product of nonnegative simplices, a fixed hidden record
before the random batch, and the same finite set of hard-budget query batches.
This note checks the optimization logic; it does not validate empirical output.

1. A primary minimax optimum can be uniformly dominated.

Take one block with E=1, coefficients c=(0,0,2,3), and budget B=1. Index
coordinates by 0,1,2,3. For a retained set S of size three, the expected-width
column is its coefficient range times its indicator.

Retained S       Column                    Bad-policy probability
{0,1,2}         (2,2,2,0)                  1/3
{0,1,3}         (3,3,0,3)                  2/9
{0,2,3}         (3,0,3,3)                  2/9
{1,2,3}         (0,3,3,3)                  2/9

The bad mixture has expected coefficients (2,2,2,2) and expected width 2
for every feasible y. The deterministic policy retaining {0,1,2} has expected
coefficients (2,2,2,0) and expected width 2-2*y_3. Thus it is never worse and
is strictly better when y_3>0. Both have worst expected width exactly 2.
The feasible hidden record y=(1/3,1/3,1/3,0) gives width 2 for every listed
batch, certifying that no randomized mixture can improve the primary optimum.
A zero-query batch has width 3 and does not affect that lower bound.

2. Notation and normalization for the proposed finite-game audit.

Let a_gi(pi)=sum_Q pi_Q*v_gi(Q) be the expected coefficient before multiplying
by the unknown target. Let w_g=E_g/W_initial and M_gi,Q=w_g*v_gi(Q), with
W_initial positive. Put z_gi(pi)=(M*pi)_gi and x_gi=y_gi/E_g for positive-energy
blocks. Then the normalized expected width is sum_gi x_gi*z_gi(pi), where
each x_g is nonnegative with sum_i x_gi=1. Consequently

  primary value F(pi) = sum_g max_i z_gi(pi),
  uniform-vertex mean H(pi) = sum_g (1/m_g)*sum_i z_gi(pi).

H is the average expected width over all product-simplex vertices under equal
weights within each block. Equivalently it is width at the interior barycenter.
It is not an empirical expectation over load shapes, a learned distribution,
or a guarantee about realized targets.

3. Policy A: exact dominance refinement of the archived policy pi_0.

Let z0=M*pi_0. Minimize H(pi) subject to

  pi>=0, sum_Q pi_Q=1,
  (M*pi)_gi-z0_gi <= d_g for all g,i,
  sum_g d_g <= 0,

where d_g is free in sign. This is an exact linear encoding of

  max_x [expected_width(pi,x)-expected_width(pi_0,x)]
       = sum_g max_i[(M*pi)_gi-z0_gi] <= 0.

Allowing negative d_g is essential: improvements in one block can compensate
for an increase in another block. Componentwise bounds M*pi<=z0 are sufficient
but not necessary for uniform dominance over the product-simplex record set.

The archived policy is feasible. If it is exactly primary-optimal, every
feasible refinement remains primary-optimal: F(pi)<=F(pi_0)=F*, and no feasible
policy can have F(pi)<F*. Minimizing H also selects a policy undominated in
expected width over all feasible records. Indeed, any further uniformly
dominating policy remains feasible by transitivity. If domination is strict
at any feasible record, its mean at the full-support barycenter is strictly
smaller (the difference is affine), contradicting optimality of H.

If pi_0 is only approximately optimal numerically, the mathematical statement
is instead that the refinement is no worse than pi_0 and therefore no worse
than its established optimality tolerance. Do not describe a floating-point
solver output as an exact machine-verifiable certificate.

4. Policy B: canonical primary-then-mean lexicographic objective.

First solve the primary game, obtaining F*. Then minimize H(pi) subject to

  pi>=0, sum_Q pi_Q=1,
  (M*pi)_gi <= t_g for all g,i,
  sum_g t_g <= F*.

For exact arithmetic this solves the primary optimum face, preserves the
minimax optimum, and is undominated over feasible records by the same
full-support-mean argument. It uses only coefficients, retained totals,
budget, and the declared coordinate symmetry; hidden target values are absent.

Policy B need not dominate the archived policy pointwise. Its observed mean
width may therefore improve or worsen. Its objective is canonical, but the
policy need not be unique: multiple mixtures or coefficient vectors can attain
both objectives. No claim of unique policy or unique certification outcomes
follows without another specified selection rule.

5. Numerical and interpretation checks required.

- Compute z0 from the actual archived mixture, not merely a rounded reported
  objective. Check simplex feasibility, nonnegative probabilities, and support.
- Independently compute both policies' worst expected width from sum of block
  maxima, and Policy A's dominance residual from sum of block difference maxima.
- A numerical F* below the attainable optimum can make stage two infeasible.
  Do not silently add slack and still call the result exact primary preservation.
  Log any tolerance or fallback and bound its effect.
- Policies use the same hard-budget batch columns and permitted information.
  Solver selection must not inspect withheld target values or certification.
- Smaller expected width, including pointwise expected-width dominance, does
  not imply nested realized intervals, improvement on every random draw, higher
  certification probability, or equal/better certification for each fixed record.
- This is a post-review sensitivity analysis of existing cases, not a new cohort.
  Retain the archived policies/results and report both refinements regardless
  of favorable or unfavorable realized outcomes.
- Lexicographic LP refinement is standard optimization methodology; the generic
  tie-breaking principle itself should not be claimed as new.

Conclusion: the proposed A and B formulations are valid. Policy A exactly
addresses avoidable pointwise expected-width inefficiency of saved mixtures;
Policy B provides a declared outcome-blind secondary objective on the minimax
optimal face. Neither promises superiority over deterministic recovery on the
observed panel or repairs the absence of field retrieval-cost measurements.
