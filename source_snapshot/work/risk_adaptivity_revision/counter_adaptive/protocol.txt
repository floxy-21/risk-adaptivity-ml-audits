COUNTER PRECISION STUDY -- FIXED BEFORE NEW GEOMETRY OR ANSWER REPLAY

This is a new retrospective protocol on previously used BLEM data and frozen
forecasts, not a preregistered trial, untouched source, or deployed service.
Three existing counter meters; all 91 existing eligible test days, in order.
Primary feasibility panel: first 20 eligible days of each meter. Remaining
71 days are a chronological continuation with identical code and thresholds.
No replacement, coefficient filtering, retraining or threshold tuning.

Audit tasks: six-hour UTC intervals [00,06), [06,12), [12,18); all six pairs
of daily persistence, ridge, histogram gradient boosting and MLP forecasts.
The forecasts were fixed at day start under the existing benchmark issue
schedule. At each cutoff, initially available within-block information is
the start/end cumulative counter (hence exact total) and the forecasts.
Prior-day target copies reveal prior-day values, not current-block values.
Next-day forecast rows are excluded by the declared intraday issue schedule.
There are no measured production arrival/retrieval times in this dataset.
The unknown hourly values are nonnegative with that total; no empirical
maximum is imposed as a physical bound. Eligibility was fixed by the old
study and requires complete monotone three-minute observations at exact UTC
endpoints. Its exclusion of natural failures limits external validity.

Permitted paid query: one of the five INTERNAL hourly cumulative registers.
Hard budget: at most two distinct registers per pair/block audit. No hourly
energy oracle or whole-gap export is supplied at that cost. This is a
specified access interface replay, not evidence of an actual billing model.
Queries cannot use hidden within-block values before their answers arrive.
The geometry phase loads only forecasts and outer endpoints/total; missing
persistence copies in the redistribution-safe export are regenerated from
their documented prior-day counter endpoints, available before day start. The
replay phase subsequently opens raw internal registers. No hidden target
enters policy construction, adversarial witnesses or threshold selection.

Primary precision: terminal width <= 0.5 times initial width W0.
Fixed sensitivity curve: 0.25, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.75 times W0.
Report zero-width cases separately and retain them in all-task coverage.
Normalize absolute MSE widths by squared TRAINING mean hourly consumption.

Policies (same information, budget and queries): exact deterministic adaptive
two-query minimax width; best deterministic batch for minimax width; optimal
randomized batch for worst expected width; uniform batch; greedy adaptive.
Greedy chooses a first cut minimizing worst width after that single cut,
then the best second cut conditional on the observed first answer. Among
greedy first-cut ties use the best two-query guarantee, then earliest cut;
this gives greedy a favorable answer-independent tie rule. Deterministic
batch ties use uniform-vertex mean width then earliest batch. Adaptive
first/second ties use earliest cut after optimizing their stated guarantee.
Mean-optimal randomized batches use a secondary uniform-vertex mean-width
LP on the minimax-optimal face, removing pointwise-dominated optima. This
does not call them precision-optimal. Numerical residuals are reported.

Geometry outputs: exact adaptive guarantee A, best deterministic batch
guarantee, optimal randomized mean width, and finite adversarial-profile
upper bounds U on every randomized batch's worst precision success.
The six all-energy-in-one-hour vertices satisfy every declared initial
constraint. U is an upper bound, not asserted to be the full precision
optimum. Export adversary weights and check all ten batches. For robust
numerical classification, widths within 1e-9 of a threshold count as batch
success (conservative upper bound); adaptive guarantee requires a margin
of at least 1e-9. Exact constructed boundary cases are checked separately.

Replay outputs: exact expectations over ten batches (no Monte Carlo),
realized precision success, terminal width, intervals excluding zero,
requested boundaries, endpoint provenance and containment of reference MSE.
Per-meter and model-pair summaries; meter-balanced overall results. Tasks
are dependent and only three meters exist: no iid task confidence interval.

Decision criterion for a substantial empirical bridge (not a theorem):
primary certified separation on >=10% of all tasks, with adaptive guarantee
and batch U<=0.7; present on all three meters and >=3 model pairs; observed
adaptive primary success exceeds each nonadaptive comparator overall and
on every meter. Also report greedy comparison without suppressing ties or
adverse results. Failure of any component is reported, not tuned away.

The old 0.9738064% acquisition result and all previous scientific outputs
remain unchanged. New results concern counter precision, not expected
requests until certification, forecast accuracy, or measured cash savings.
