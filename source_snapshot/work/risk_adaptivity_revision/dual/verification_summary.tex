\paragraph{Numerical hardest-completion certificates.}
We extracted a dual completion from the unchanged compact solver for all
28 frozen scaling instances, the 42 earlier small verification instances,
four separated-cluster examples, and six declared edge cases (80 instances
in total). The completed nonnegative masses satisfy the residual block
totals with maximum absolute sum error $1.78\times10^{-15}$. The existing
weighted pricing oracle computes $\min_Q W(Q,y^*)$; its maximum difference
from the compact primal is $2.14\times10^{-14}$ in absolute units, or
$4.62\times10^{-16}$ after division by
$\max(1,\hbox{initial worst-case width})$. Independently enumerating every
coordinate subset of size at most the hard budget on 64 small instances
(including 12 scaling instances) checked 17,749 terminal sets. These
include shorter batches and redundant full-block queries. The maximum
normalized enumeration--primal discrepancy is $2.74\times10^{-16}$.
The LP primal and dual feasibility tolerances are $10^{-9}$; the checking
threshold is $10^{-8}$ under the stated objective or block-total scaling.
No negative multiplier required clipping; the largest removed numerical
excess of a block's mass was $8.89\times10^{-16}$. Genuine dual mass
deficits are completed at a fixed coordinate and recorded separately.
The supplement reports every original-order completion, raw multiplier,
correction, pricing minimizer, exhaustive minimum, and source hash.
These are ordinary floating-point checks, not outward-rounded
certificates or additional empirical forecasting results.
