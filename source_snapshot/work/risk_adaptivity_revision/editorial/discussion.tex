\section{Operational interpretation and scope}
The classification turns the choice of acquisition objective into a
decision about feedback. A mean-optimal batch suffices for every
nondecreasing concave width penalty; a precision requirement can instead
make feedback necessary. The cumulative-query algorithm determines which
situation applies before opening optional measurements.

\subsection{A deployment decision, including when to avoid feedback}
An implementation first records the available archive, valid physical or
measurement constraints, and actual retrieval interface. It then computes
the initial interval and compares it with the required precision. If the
initial interval suffices, no retrieval is needed. Otherwise a guaranteeing
fixed batch avoids a feedback dependency. If only the adaptive rule meets
the requirement at the stated budget, feedback is necessary for that
deterministic assurance. If neither meets it, the rule reports the missing
guarantee rather than treating a point estimate as certified. A bulk export
or a different cost model must be evaluated alongside these choices.

The capacity experiment illustrates why this information check is essential.
At the unchanged absolute requirement, an illustrative $E/2$ cap changes
282 of 290 originally feedback-essential cases into fixed-batch cases;
an $E/3$ cap changes all 290. These restrictions supply extra information.
Their value is that less acquisition assurance is needed, not evidence
that the uncapped algorithm fails. Neither cap is a validated physical
limit, and each excludes some recorded traces. The paper therefore does
not count those hypothetical bounds as deployed operational improvements.
Likewise the measured public API favors bulk retrieval over selective
requests. The complete decision process must retain this alternative.

\subsection{Mathematical and measurement boundaries}
The universal result requires a known compact convex uncertainty set,
scalar affine target, finite exact-linear query dictionary and the same
hard budget for both policy classes. Valid bounds, overlapping constraints
and unequal fixed costs are allowed. A fixed-error latent-record lift
extends this formulation to declared bounded measurement errors
(Appendix~\ref{app:stored-error}); its guarantees concern that enlarged
model. Error bounds and acquisition timestamps must be justified at
deployment, and the noisy general LP is not the compact exact-total solver.

The efficient coordinate specialization uses disjoint exact totals and
unit query costs. The counter rule uses two prefix queries and a known
nonnegative total, with a quadratic arithmetic-operation bound for any
block length. The compact formulation does not provide an efficient
arbitrary-budget precision solver for general polytopes. Classical
minimax theory, Jensen's inequality, Helly theory, local information
diameter and polyhedral dynamic programming supply established tools;
the claims concern the penalty classification, query-realizable sharp
separations and their computable consequences.

\subsection{Empirical strength and its limits}
Feedback-essential prevalence is 5.90\% in the original panel, below its
prespecified 10\% target, and 1.73\% in the external panel. Overall
recorded gains over the best fixed deterministic worst-width batch are
0.407 and 0.119 percentage points. Greedy adaptation attains 100\%
precision on the external 29-case subset. The exact planner's theoretical
optimality therefore does not establish a large recorded advantage over
ordinary adaptation. Bounded internal-readout sensitivity provides exact rational certificates of the adaptive guarantee
in 22 of the 29 external cases at an allowance of 1\% of
block total, with exact endpoints. That check imposes no hourly cap; its
allowances are not calibrated device specifications. The half-width requirement is a declared scale-free
service level, not a calibrated financial decision threshold. Three
systems per source and dependent model pairs do not establish population
prevalence.

A narrow interval need not certify a winner; the paired ordering results
and adverse historical controls address that distinction separately.
Expected requests until certification, shared costs across pairs and
monetary savings require other objectives and measurements. The present
result supports an assurance decision under the specified information model.

\section{Conclusion}
Concavity characterizes when randomized feedback is universally irrelevant
to minimax penalties of sharp scalar width. Nonconcave penalties admit
strict coordinate-query advantages and growing sharp separations.
A dimension-dependent Helly consequence and an exact two-prefix algorithm
turn the distinction into a decision about attainable precision.
Matching witnesses establish exact half-success gaps on two public-data
sources. Capacity and actual-interface tests identify when that gap ceases
to justify feedback. The resulting method selects an assurance-compatible
acquisition protocol while making its information, budget and measurement
assumptions explicit.
