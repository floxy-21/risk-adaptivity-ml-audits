\section{Discussion}
\subsection{The decision supported by a precision certificate}
The practical output is a choice of acquisition protocol for a specified
uncertainty budget. The auditor supplies the model contrasts, available
information, permissible measurements and precision target. A sufficient
initial interval needs no query; otherwise the certificate identifies a
fixed or adaptive route, or the need for more information.

Precision and model selection answer different questions. On the external
panel, adaptation certifies a best model on 207 blocks and greedy on 208,
despite adaptation's higher joint-precision success. The original-panel
counts are 123 and 118; fixed batches certify 122 original and 201 external
blocks. Narrowing every comparison therefore need not identify more winners.
The half-width target is a declared reporting requirement, not a validated
threshold for model replacement or financial action. Requests until a winner
is identified require a different optimization objective.

\subsection{What sharing the budget changes}
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-target-collapse} shows that two simultaneous comparisons
can remove the scalar two-query success floor. Adaptive certainty coexists
with optimal batch success $1/m$, although each contrast separately admits
a sufficient fixed batch. The dictionary grows, the normalized threshold
margin shrinks, and three fixed readings suffice. The result concerns
assurance at the same budget, not an unbounded reduction in readings.

The meter certificates establish a narrower counterpart on actual frozen
forecast geometries: with two readings shared across six comparisons,
adaptation guarantees precision on 165 original and 57 external blocks
where the best randomized batch has worst-case success exactly one half.
The worked example gives both normalized and original loss-unit widths
(Table~\ref{tab:absolute_joint_widths}). Across all blocks, recorded gains
over fixed worst-width batching are 7.81 and 11.11 percentage points, versus
2.20 and 2.15 over greedy adaptation. The separate-pair gains are only
0.407 and 0.119 points. Each source contains three systems, and the
shared-budget extension reuses these panels retrospectively.

Additional information changes the acquisition decision. Capacity bounds
can remove the need for feedback; errors can increase the sufficient budget.
These controls use declared sensitivity models, with exact retained
endpoints, rather than calibrated physical measurement bounds
(Table~\ref{tab:measurement-assumptions}). Recorded performance also differs
from worst-case assurance: a covering mixture can have a better guarantee
yet succeed less often on the stored traces. The 351 original and 67 external
open batch-precision gaps restrict exact superiority claims to the instances
with matching certificates.

\subsection{When the retrieval interface favors another policy}
Bulk retrieval is the best observed option on the measured three-task
public-API workload: it uses one request, takes less I/O time and removes
all remaining width. The two-reading contract defines a different,
restricted benchmark. The protocol selector chooses among supplied
certificates using reading and feedback-round limits; it does not infer
that an unlisted protocol is impossible. The separate 29-case cost study
also exposes the tradeoff: 65 reserved readings and 51 rounds for adaptation
with fixed fallback, versus 87 readings and 29 rounds for fixed retrieval.
That is a declared-cost comparison, not measured deployment savings.

The finite measurement dictionary is essential. If every linear functional
were available at unit cost, one could query a scalar target directly.
The main exact planner covers two cumulative-prefix queries with a known
nonnegative total. General convex-set results, unequal fixed costs and
bounded-error extensions have wider mathematical scope, but do not provide
an efficient arbitrary-budget precision optimizer. Online Resource~1 retains
the scalar penalty classification (S11), the separate mean-joint-width
construction (S14) and the specialized coordinate-recovery solver (S5/S7).

\section{Conclusions}
Sharing a reading budget can make feedback necessary for an assurance
requirement that each comparison could meet separately. With two affine
targets and two coordinate queries, adaptive success can be one while
optimal randomized-batch success is $1/m$. The same construction covers
all pairs among three models.

An exact prefix-query planner and checkable certificates connect this
boundary to model auditing: 165 original and 57 external meter blocks admit
adaptive assurance one and optimal batch assurance one half under the
specified two-reading contract. Recorded gains, uncertainty controls and
the bulk-retrieval comparison delimit that benefit. The implementation
supports a conditional acquisition decision; bulk is preferable on the
measured unrestricted API, and precision alone does not establish a model
winner.
