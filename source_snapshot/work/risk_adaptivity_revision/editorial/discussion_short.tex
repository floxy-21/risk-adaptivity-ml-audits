\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Planning the readings needed for assurance}
The auditor supplies the comparisons, available information and permissible
measurements. Our method returns the minimum sufficient fixed reading budget
and checks whether feedback reduces it. The reading budget is an output of this calculation, so the auditor need
not first impose a two-reading quota. Each saving exchanges one scalar
reading for an additional information round. With declared charges
$c_{\rm read}>0$ per reserved reading and $c_{\rm round}\geq0$ per
round, the two assured plans cost $3c_{\rm read}+c_{\rm round}$ and
$2c_{\rm read}+2c_{\rm round}$. Feedback is preferable exactly when
$c_{\rm round}<c_{\rm read}$; ties favor the one-round fixed plan.
This is an analytic choice between these plans under the same exact
information model. An admissible bulk action is compared separately.

Theorem~\ref{thm:two-target-collapse} explains why sharing readings matters.
Two simultaneous comparisons can remove the scalar two-query success floor:
adaptive certainty coexists with optimal batch success $1/m$, although each
contrast separately admits a sufficient fixed batch. The adverse records
force all $m$ batches even for positive worst-record success and identify the
unique optimal mixture. The output dimension and query budget stay fixed as
the dictionary grows; three fixed readings suffice in this construction.

The scalar-budget checks connect this construction to real forecast
coefficients: most reading-saving blocks are already certifiable pair by
pair, but require feedback when the readings must serve every pair.
The exact threshold intervals establish where this conclusion remains
stable. Recorded gains are larger over fixed batching than over greedy
adaptation; they describe performance on these panels rather than the
worst-case separation proved by the certificates.

Absolute precision changes which readings are worth acquiring. At the
declared training-scaled floor, all 57 external cases retain assurance with
at most two readings, but 39 can use a simpler fixed plan. The floor-aware
planner therefore reserves 70 readings rather than the minimum fixed total
of 88 within this unchanged cohort. Across the complete external panel the
reduction is 5.29\%. The half-width fraction and absolute floor are
reporting targets. Relating them to a financial materiality threshold
requires application-specific calibration.

\subsection{Scope and protocol choice}
The main planner uses two cumulative-prefix queries and an exact
nonnegative total. Its reading budget measures disclosed scalar values;
the API control measures requests and I/O time. An unrestricted range
request returns the complete record faster on the tested interface, so
bulk is preferable when admissible. Online Resource~1, S16 evaluates a
separate stopping rule for tolerance-based model choice. With endpoints
already retained, it certifies an acceptable model before fetching finer
data on 8/819 original and 198/279 external blocks. Its system-level
loss-scale table explains the tolerance; cold-start and whole-period
comparisons account for endpoint setup and range batching. This is a
supplementary acquisition option, distinct from joint-precision planning.

Additional information can change the plan. Of the 225 baseline saving
blocks, 75 are excluded by the stronger $E/3$ cap and all 150 compatible
blocks become fixed-certifiable. Thus capacity removes the feedback
requirement on those same records, independently of the exclusion effect.
Bounded errors can increase the sufficient reading budget.
Table~\ref{tab:measurement-assumptions} states these sensitivity models,
including exact retained endpoints and uncalibrated error allowances.
The source panels each contain three systems and were reused retrospectively.
The descriptive intervals summarize these panels; dependence beyond the
resampled calendar blocks remains unmodeled. Population generalization
requires additional systems.

Precision limits how much an audit's reported loss difference can change
when the missing readings arrive. A model decision also depends on the
intervals' positions. At the training-scaled tolerance, adaptive and greedy
querying each certify an acceptable model on 277 external blocks, versus
263 for fixed batching. The strict-winner counts favor greedy by one block.
These outcomes distinguish the benefit
of feedback from the value of exact planning for the precision requirement.
The tolerance and charges remain choices for an audit service; the study
measures data requirements and API behavior, not a deployed financial return.
Online Resource~1 contains the
broader scalar theory, coordinate-recovery solver and protocol-selection
cost sensitivity. A next step is to develop scalable planners for larger
adaptive reading budgets.

\section{Conclusions}
Sharing a reading budget can make feedback necessary for an assurance
requirement that each comparison can meet separately. With two affine
targets and two coordinate queries, adaptive success can be one while
optimal randomized-batch success is $1/m$. The same construction covers
all pairs among three models.

Our exact planner and verifiable certificates turn this boundary into a
reading-budget decision. The meter study establishes where two adaptive
readings replace the minimum three fixed readings and isolates the
shared-budget mechanism through separate scalar checks. Absolute-scale
and capacity analyses identify where fixed readings suffice instead.
The resulting plans specify the readings and feedback required for a stated
assurance level, alongside the option of an admissible bulk export.
