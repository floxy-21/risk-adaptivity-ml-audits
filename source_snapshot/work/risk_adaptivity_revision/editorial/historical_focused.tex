\subsection{Complementary archive certification and adverse controls}
\label{sec:historical-application}
The historical archive study supplies a complementary use of the same
sharp interval: authenticate a model ranking under incomplete verification
data, without further acquisition. It reuses four frozen forecasters on
30 Portugal consumption series, one French household and three cumulative
counters (Appendix~\ref{app:historical-design}). Every permitted persistence
copy $p^{\rm pers}_{t+24}=y_t$ and exact-total deduction is incorporated
before every comparator. The complete retrospective archive admits later
rows within its evaluation period; its information boundary differs from
the intraday counter audit. Whole-period endpoints average equal-length
daily endpoints after this closure, over the resulting product feasible set.

On the complete Portugal archive-aware exact-total grid, direct paired
bounds certify 7,836 of 9,720 comparisons (80.62\%), versus 2,513
(25.85\%) from separately bounded individual losses. Both receive identical
information and zero new readings. Separate subtraction can combine loss
extrema attained at different target records, whereas pairing enforces a
common completion. This is the reason for the additional certificates,
not a newly claimed loss identity or improvement in prediction accuracy.
For exact totals, individual-loss minima are simplex projections and maxima
occur at simplex vertices. For a nonnegative total band, the minimum is
projection onto that band (possibly at an interior total), and the maximum
occurs at a band-polytope vertex.

An implemented gate also authenticates the historical donor's selected
model only when it beats each of the three alternatives for every compatible
record. Pairing authenticates 933 of 1,620 Portugal proposals, versus 167
using separate-loss bounds: a 47.28-percentage-point difference and
52.72\% fewer unresolved proposals. Its descriptive 95\% interval,
40.80--53.77 points, uses 2,000 whole-consumer percentile-bootstrap draws,
\texttt{default\_rng(20261012)}, and linear quantile interpolation; all
54 scenarios of a consumer remain together. It does not estimate
shared-calendar or population uncertainty. On the existing 30 additional
consumers, the full grid certifies 7,753 versus 2,527 comparisons with exact
totals and 7,424 versus 2,010 with 5\% total tolerance, out of 9,720 each.
These are repeated scenarios from previously examined consumers, not a new
source or independent evidence for an adaptive precision advantage.

The adverse controls are consequential. The donor makes no observed
whole-period errors at full coverage on the 9,720 Portugal, 324 household
and 972 counter exact-total comparisons; the certificate adds robustness
to compatible completions, not demonstrated accuracy over that imputer.
In the original Portugal six-hour archive, free copies recover 95.33\%
of masked targets. Daily certification rises from 96.67\% to 98.71\% after
two readings per pair, but exact selection adds only 0.042 percentage points
over greedy. Robust randomization reduces its worst-width objective from
29.595\% to 26.673\% of initial width while increasing realized expected
width from 20.154\% to 23.670\% and reducing certification from 98.795\%
to 98.688\%. Both outcome-independent optimal-face refinements in
Section~\ref{sec:tie-selection} retain that adverse tradeoff within tolerance.
The earlier decision-acquisition experiment's 0.9738064\% mean saving is
unchanged, with its original denominator. None of these results establishes
operational retrieval savings or the superiority of mean-width optimization
for precision. They motivate the present distinction between risk objectives.

Online Resource~1 retains the complete 324-cell historical grid, its 18
aggregates, all wider-total and four-model results, raw-source instructions,
and executable gate examples with attaining endpoint completions. Independent
endpoint LPs and a separately written paired/separate-bound checker verify
the reported comparisons; their tolerances and complete outputs are mapped
in \texttt{CURRENT\_RESULTS\_MAP.txt}. The main text uses these results as
complementary controls, keeping the counter study as the application of the
adaptive precision theorem.
