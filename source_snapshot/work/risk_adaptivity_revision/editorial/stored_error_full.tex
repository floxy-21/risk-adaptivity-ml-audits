
\paragraph{Certified bounded stored-readout sensitivity.}\label{sec:stored-error-sensitivity}
We retain all 29 external half-separation cases and ask whether feedback's
assurance advantage survives errors in the retrieved internal readings.
Normalize the retained total to one and divide the original coefficient
vector by its positive range, without translation. Certificate-LP widths
are then measured in units of the original $W_0$, with initial width one.
The hidden record consists of
$y_h\geq0$, $\sum_h y_h=1$, and five fixed errors
$e_k\in[-\delta,\delta]$; query $k$ returns
$\sum_{h\leq k}y_h+e_k$. Errors are fixed before policy randomization.
The endpoints remain exact, and no hourly cap is imposed in this separate
sensitivity calculation. Before solving, we fixed
$\delta\in\{0,0.001,0.01\}$ and the unchanged requirement $W\leq W_0/2$.
Initial width remains $W_0$, so halving the new initial width gives the
same threshold. These allowances concern block-total units; they are
illustrative bounds, not calibrated physical-device specifications.

The positive-error guarantees in Table~\ref{tab:stored-error-certificates}
are established by exact rational upper certificates, not a numerical
threshold tolerance. For each of the 49 successful case--allowance
combinations, retain its first query and at most two successors. Write
its shared-answer endpoint LP in minimum form as $f^\top z=-\eta$,
subject to $Az\leq b$, $Hz=h$ and valid finite bounds $L\leq z\leq U$.
For rational multipliers $\lambda\leq0$ and unrestricted $\nu$, put
$r=f-A^\top\lambda-H^\top\nu$. Standard weak duality with a box correction gives
\[
 f^\top z\ \geq\ \lambda^\top b+\nu^\top h+
       \sum_j\min\{r_jL_j,r_jU_j\}=:\ell.
\]
Thus $-\ell$ rigorously bounds that support-defined adaptive policy's
worst width, even without exact stationarity of the proposed multipliers.
The boxes follow from the model: increments lie in $[0,1]$, errors in
$[-\delta,\delta]$, $\eta\in[0,1]$, and the first answer in
$[-\delta,1+\delta]$. Two solver-free implementations reconstruct the LPs
from the original serialized coefficient fractions and verify every bound.
All 49 exact upper bounds are below $1/2$; the smallest certified margin
exceeds $0.003759W_0$. This certifies a policy that chooses an exact
conditional-width minimizer from its saved successor support, without
claiming an exact optimal-width value. The $0.01E$ fallback below additionally
certifies executable branching.

The original exact adverse-profile upper bound $S_B^\star\leq1/2$ remains
valid because every zero-error record and its old fiber are included in
the enlarged model. The certified two-successor policy supplies the
matching all-completions batch lower guarantee by the scalar-first support
result. Consequently the exact success separation survives in the stated
27 and 22 cases, including every system and model pair. No deterministic
batch guarantees the threshold on any of the original 29 cases at these
error levels. Independent error-eliminated LPs also reproduce all 87
case--level calculations, with maximum discrepancy $1.89\times10^{-15}$.
The complete ledger and 49 rational upper certificates remain available.

The remaining two cases at $0.001E$ and seven at $0.01E$ are now resolved
by separate exact lower witnesses. For every legal first query $k$, a
rational answer $t_k$ is supplied; for each possible second query $j$, two
feasible latent records share first answer $t_k$ and an identical second
answer, while their target contrast exceeds $W_0/2$. A deterministic policy
must choose one such $j$ after $t_k$, so some permitted record leaves its
terminal interval wider than the requirement. Stopping earlier gives a
coarser fiber. Two solver-free checks verify all 45 first-query witnesses
and 180 endpoint pairs, with smallest strict excess greater than
$5.9060\times10^{-6}W_0$. Thus no deterministic policy with the same
two-query budget can guarantee the threshold in those cases. This lower
bound does not assert impossibility for other budgets or policy objectives.
The corresponding numerical optimal widths at $0.01E$ range from
$0.500005906W_0$ to $0.530000000W_0$. The difference from 29/29 at zero error
is a loss of attainable assurance as the observational fibers expand,
rather than a missing successful certificate or an LP tie-breaking choice.

\paragraph{A certified budget fallback covering all 29 cases.}
A separate post-analysis calculation keeps the same cases, $0.01E$ error
allowance, exact endpoints and $W_0/2$ requirement, but permits a third
reading when two are insufficient. It enumerates all 31 nonempty fixed
batches per case (899 LPs). Exact rational upper certificates verify a
three-reading fixed batch for every case; the largest certified bound is
$0.458896W_0$. The acquisition rule selects a previously certified
two-query adaptive support for the 22 cases, and a certified three-query
fixed batch for the other seven, using only coefficients and initial
constraints before retrieval. It therefore guarantees the requirement in
all 29 cases with maximum budget three.

For the 22 two-reading prescriptions, rational conditional dual bounds
give affine upper bounds indexed by possible second queries. Their lower
envelope is at most $W_0/2$ on the entire first-answer interval
$[-0.01,1.01]$, verified at every endpoint and line intersection. The
implementation selects a minimizing bound by exact rational comparisons,
then requests that second reading; no online LP is needed. This branch
rule has the required assurance but need not select the same branch as
the original conditional-width minimizer, so the earlier recorded replay
figures are not attributed to it. Separate tests cover 1,015 executions on
constructed feasible records and reject out-of-model transcripts; the
continuous guarantee follows from the exact certificates, not these tests.
