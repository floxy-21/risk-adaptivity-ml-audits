\section{Compact Recovery with Disjoint Aggregate Totals}
\label{sec:structured}
The general theory applies to any compact convex $F$, but efficient
optimization still depends on its geometry. Consider residual targets
$y_{gi}\geq0$ in disjoint blocks $\mathcal M_g$, with exact sums
$\sum_i y_{gi}=E_g$. Here the intercept $d$ includes all fixed observed
contributions to the contrast; only unknown coordinates remain in the
residual notation. Write
$\F=\prod_g\{y_g\geq0:\sum_i y_{gi}=E_g\}$ for this residual
representation of the full feasible record $F$. The sharp paired interval has endpoints
\begin{equation}
 D^-=d+\sum_g E_g\min_{i\in\mathcal M_g}c_{gi},\qquad
 D^+=d+\sum_g E_g\max_{i\in\mathcal M_g}c_{gi}.
 \label{eq:sharp}
\end{equation}
Blocks with no missing coordinates are omitted after checking that their
observed sums equal the recorded totals.
Each extremum assigns the block's mass to an extremal coefficient;
disjointness permits simultaneous attainment. A singleton is determined
by its total, and a zero-total block is entirely determined. Negative
residual totals indicate inconsistent input rather than a valid feasible
set. A recorded counter increment must refer to the same units and
interval as the targets; an approximate physical total requires an
appropriately widened constraint.

For queried coordinates $Q_g$ and retained set $S_g=\mathcal M_g\setminus Q_g$,
\begin{equation}
 W(Q,y)=\sum_g\left(\sum_{i\in S_g}y_{gi}\right)
                  \operatorname{range}(c_{g,S_g}).
 \label{eq:querywidth}
\end{equation}
The range of an empty set or singleton is zero. In every block with a
nonempty retained set, all mass can be assigned to unqueried coordinates;
a fully queried block contributes zero width. Thus the worst width of a fixed batch is
$\sum_g E_g\operatorname{range}(c_{g,S_g})$.

\subsection{Deterministic recovery as a reference}
Sort the $m_g$ coefficients in a block. Retaining $m_g-k$ coefficients
with minimum range is achieved by a consecutive window, giving
\begin{equation}
 h_g(k)=E_g\min_{1\leq s\leq k+1}
       (c_{g,(s+m_g-k-1)}-c_{g,(s)}),\quad 0\leq k\leq m_g-1.
 \label{eq:window}
\end{equation}
Every retained subset spans a window of that size with no larger range,
which proves the reduction. Allocate queries by
\begin{equation}
 D_B=\min_{\sum_g k_g\leq B}\sum_g h_g(k_g),\qquad
 V(g,b)=\min_k\{V(g-1,b-k)+h_g(k)\}.
 \label{eq:allocation}
\end{equation}
The recurrence minimizes over $0\leq k\leq\min(b,m_g-1)$, has
$V(0,0)=0$ and infeasible states equal to infinity, and extracts
$D_B=\min_{0\leq b\leq B}V(G,b)$ for the at-most budget.
It returns the queried complements of the selected windows. The budget
is a count of distinct acquired coordinates for one contrast, not a
measured monetary cost or a shared budget over all pairs of models.

\begin{proposition}[Deterministic adaptive reference]
\label{prop:det-adapt}
For nonnegative residual targets with disjoint exact totals and unit
costs, the deterministic adaptive worst-width optimum equals $D_B$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Omit queries whose values are already determined and simulate their
answers in the policy's history. Answer zero to every remaining query.
Every positive-total block retains an unqueried coordinate on this
transcript; assign its mass to such a coordinate at termination. This is
a feasible completion realizing the transcript. Its terminal width is
the worst width of the terminal batch and is at least $D_B$. The optimal
fixed batch supplies the reverse inequality.
\end{proof}
