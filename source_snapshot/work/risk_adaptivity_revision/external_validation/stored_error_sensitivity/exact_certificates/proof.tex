\paragraph{Exact upper certificates for the positive-error cases.}
For each of the 27 and 22 positive-allowance survivors, retain its computed
first query and at most two successor queries. With normalized total one,
the shared-answer endpoint LP minimizes $f^\top z=-\eta$ under
$Az\leq b$, $Hz=h$ and a finite box $L\leq z\leq U$.
Its maximum $\eta$ is the worst width of the policy selecting an exact
minimizer of conditional width from this successor support.
The implied boxes are $x_h\in[0,1]$, $e_k\in[-\delta,\delta]$,
$\eta\in[0,1]$ and first answer $t\in[-\delta,1+\delta]$.
They preserve the optimum: feasible widths are nonnegative and at most the
normalized initial width one, and every conditional endpoint pair can
coincide to permit $\eta=0$.

Take any rational multipliers $\lambda\leq0$ and unrestricted $\nu$, and set
$r=f-A^\top\lambda-H^\top\nu$. Every feasible point satisfies
\[
 f^\top z\geq \lambda^\top b+\nu^\top h+
       \sum_j\min\{r_jL_j,r_jU_j\}=:\ell.
\]
Indeed, $\lambda^\top Az\geq\lambda^\top b$,
$\nu^\top Hz=\nu^\top h$, and each residual coordinate is bounded by
its box minimum. Thus $-\ell$ is a rigorous upper bound on worst width,
even when the proposed multipliers do not satisfy exact stationarity.
We convert solver multipliers to exact binary64 fractions, clip inequality
multipliers to the nonpositive half-line, reconstruct the original serialized
coefficients and positive normalization exactly, and compute this correction
in rational arithmetic.

All 49 bounds are strictly below $1/2$, with smallest certified margin
$0.00375922734$ in original-width units. A separate solver-free checker
reconstructs every matrix and verifies the inequalities using fractions.
The existing exact adverse-completion upper bound on randomized-batch
success remains $1/2$ by inclusion of the zero-error records and fibers;
the certified two-successor policy supplies the matching continuum lower
guarantee. This certifies the same 27 and 22 cases under the declared
sensitivity model. It neither asserts an exact optimal-width value nor
tests a floating-point online branching implementation or a physical
