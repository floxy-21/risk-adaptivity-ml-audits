\paragraph{Exact diagnosis of the failed deterministic guarantees.}
Normalize the retained total to one and let
\[
 F_\delta=\{(x,e):x\in\mathbb R_+^6,\ \mathbf1^Tx=1,
                         \ e\in[-\delta,\delta]^5\}.
\]
The permitted query $k$ returns $q_k(x,e)=\sum_{h\le k}x_h+e_k$;
the error vector is a fixed part of the hidden record.
Write $\widetilde c=c/(\max c-\min c)$, so the precision threshold,
in units of the original width, is $1/2$.
For each first query $k$, the supplied witness specifies a rational answer $t_k$.
For every $j\ne k$, it supplies two records $z_{kj}^+,z_{kj}^-\in F_\delta$
such that
\[
 q_k(z_{kj}^+)=q_k(z_{kj}^-)=t_k,\qquad
 q_j(z_{kj}^+)=q_j(z_{kj}^-),\qquad
 \widetilde c^T(x_{kj}^+-x_{kj}^-)>\tfrac12.
\]
Fix any deterministic policy with at most two queries. Its first query is some
$k$; after answer $t_k$, its second query and stopping decision are determined.
If it chooses $j$, the two corresponding records produce the same complete
transcript, and the sharp terminal interval contains both contrast values.
Its width therefore exceeds $1/2$. If the policy stops after the first query,
or repeats that query, its information is weaker and the same obstruction
remains. Checking all five first choices proves that no such deterministic
policy guarantees the threshold. Different policies may have different
adverse records: this argument does not establish a lower bound against
randomized adaptive policies whose hidden record must precede their random seed.

All nine previously failed case--allowance combinations admit these witnesses:
two at $\delta=10^{-3}$ and seven at $\delta=10^{-2}$.
Their certified optimal deterministic widths exceed $1/2$ by at least
$5.9060297954\times10^{-6}$; the smallest exact excess is
$231625733590097946267/39218517618895276000000000$.
There are 45 first-query witnesses and 180 indistinguishable endpoint pairs.
All feasibility, transcript and strict-width inequalities are checked with
rational arithmetic using the exact fractions represented by the frozen
binary64 forecast coefficients. This certifies failure of the deterministic
precision requirement under the declared illustrative model, rather than
merely failure of a floating-point solver to find a certificate. It does not
validate the chosen allowances as physical meter errors.
