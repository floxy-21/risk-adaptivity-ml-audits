FINITE-WITNESS TEST FOR TWO-QUERY ASSURANCE

Status: elementary consequence of the finite query dictionary, not a claim
of a new general theorem or an optimal randomized-adaptive value.

Model and premise
-----------------
The latent record is fixed before an independent policy seed is drawn.
There are K legal first queries. After first query k, there are J_k legal
second actions; these dictionaries and their costs do not depend on answers.
Every execution uses at most two queries. Success requires every terminal
sharp target interval to meet its fixed width threshold. Query and stopping
rules are measurable. The current exact-total simplex has five internal
prefix queries and four distinct continuations for each first query.

Suppose no deterministic adaptive two-query policy guarantees success.
For every first query k there is a feasible answer t_k at which no second
action guarantees success over the compatible records. In the present model
this follows directly from the exact conditional success intervals: if their
finite union covered the answer domain, selecting the least-index successful
action would give a legal measurable deterministic guaranteeing policy.
The constructed intervals are closed rational intervals, so this selector
has no measurability ambiguity.

For each legal pair (k,j), choose a feasible record x_kj giving first answer
t_k, on which terminal batch {k,j} fails. Such a record exists by the premise.
Our implementation supplies two explicit simplex endpoints u_kj and v_kj
with identical k and j prefix readings and a contrast difference strictly
larger than one half of its initial range. Set x_kj=v_kj. The sharp fiber
contains both endpoints, so the target fails regardless of post-processing.

Seed-independent adverse mixture
--------------------------------
Let N=sum_k J_k. Put mass 1/N on each ordered witness x_kj, independently of
the policy seed. Coincident records retain their multiplicities. This is a
fixed hidden-record distribution, not an adversary responding to queries.

For any randomized adaptive policy P, denote its first action by K(omega)
and its second action after first answer t by J(k,t,omega). Every x_kj in
row k gives the same t_k when the first action is k. Consequently

 E_{x~mu,omega}[failure of P on x]
 >= (1/N) sum_k sum_j Pr_omega[K(omega)=k, J(k,t_k,omega)=j]
  = (1/N) sum_k Pr_omega[K(omega)=k]
  = 1/N.

The restriction of the seed to first action k is identical across records
in that row; no record-dependent reconditioning is used. Other failures
only increase the left side. Thus every randomized adaptive policy has a
fixed record on which success is at most 1-1/N, and the supremum over all
such policies also obeys this bound. The result rules out even a sequence
of randomized policies whose worst-record success approaches one.

Stopping early and repeated queries
----------------------------------
Pad a shorter execution with legal distinct queries without using later
answers to revise its previous choices. In the current five-prefix model,
there is always an available continuation and padding respects budget two.
Additional observations can only shrink sharp intervals, so failure after
padding implies failure of the original execution. The upper bound for the
padded class therefore also bounds stopping/repetition policies. A no-query
execution can first be padded with any fixed first query and continuation.

Consequence for the existing joint panels
---------------------------------------
Here K=5 and J_k=4, so N=20 and S_adaptive <= 19/20 on every instance with
no deterministic two-query guarantee. The bound is not asserted tight.
Conversely, an available deterministic sure policy is itself a randomized
policy with a degenerate seed. Certainty feasibility is therefore equivalent
for deterministic and randomized adaptive policies in this declared model.

The supplied exact check reconstructs all first-query success intervals from
the source-derived serialized coefficients. It constructs and directly checks
twenty endpoint witnesses for each excluded instance: 489 original and 94
external cases. All witnesses are synthetic normalized simplex points.
Neither recorded outcomes nor the empirical prevalence of adverse profiles
enters the proof or construction.

Interpretation
--------------
The exact three-way protocol classification can be stated as: fixed certainty
is available; feedback is necessary for certainty within the two-reading
class; or no two-reading protocol, including randomized adaptation, can meet
certainty. This is a feasibility result under a declared uncertainty set,
threshold and access dictionary. It supplies neither a deployment budget
justification nor improved model selection, privacy/compliance assurance,
physical-meter calibration, monetary savings, or an optimal success value
below one. A catalogue exclusion without these witnesses remains insufficient.
