\paragraph{Transfer from precision to a model decision.}
We also replayed the frozen policies against a decision criterion: certify
a model whose six-hour MSE exceeds that of any other candidate by at most
$\epsilon$. For each model, we take the maximum of zero and the three directed
upper loss-difference bounds; reversing a stored comparison uses minus its
lower endpoint. We certify the model when this maximum is at most $\epsilon$.
The primary retrospective tolerance is $\epsilon=0.01M_2$, using the same
training second moment as the absolute-floor diagnostic. The supplement
reports all seven factors from zero to $0.1$, without changing the acquisition
policies.

On the external panel, adaptation adds 14 tolerance certificates over the
fixed batch and loses none: a 5.02-percentage-point gain (descriptive 95\%
calendar-block bootstrap interval, 2.51--8.60). Twelve of these cases are from
HPM~2 and two from HPM~1. The original-panel gain is 0.12 points
($-1.34$--1.71). Adaptive and greedy certify the same 277 external blocks;
the original difference is 0.37 points ($-0.37$--1.10).
Thus some precision gains transfer to a model decision, with the clearest
observed benefit over fixed batching in the external panel. The tolerance
grid is a sensitivity analysis; it is not a calibrated deployment threshold.
The intervals use 2,000 synchronized seven-day circular calendar-block
resamples, with seeds 20261015 and 20261016 for the original and external
panels, respectively.
