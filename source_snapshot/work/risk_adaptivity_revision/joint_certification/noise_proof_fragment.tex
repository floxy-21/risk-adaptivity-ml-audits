\subsection{Shared certificates with bounded stored-register errors}
\label{sec:joint-stored-errors}
This sensitivity calculation retains the same two shared prefix queries and
the original precision target $J\leq 1/2$.  Let $x_h=y_h/E$, so that
$x\geq0$ and $\sum_hx_h=1$, and let $P_kx=\sum_{h\leq k}x_h$.
The six normalized contrasts are obtained by subtracting each contrast's
minimum coefficient and dividing by its coefficient range.  Hence $J$ is
the largest of the six sharp widths divided by its original, uncapped,
zero-query width.  Optional capacity information imposes $x_h\leq\alpha$;
it does not change this denominator.

The stored answer at boundary $k$ is $z_k=P_kx+e_k$, where the five errors
are fixed before the policy's random choices and $|e_k|\leq\delta$.
Endpoints remain exact.  We use $\delta=0.01$, with $\alpha=1$ or $1/2$.
These are declared uncertainty sets, not measured device specifications.
The first query and piecewise second-query rule are frozen from the
coefficient-only noiseless planner.  A first answer outside its noiseless
range uses the nearest endpoint action.  At a shared boundary the first
matching closed interval is used.  We separately evaluate the original
planner and the already computed capacity-aware planner; neither is claimed
optimal for the noisy problem.

\paragraph{An all-record upper certificate.}
For a branch that queries $k$ first and $j$ second when the first answer
belongs to $[l,u]$, solve, for every normalized contrast $c_r$,
\[
 \max c_r^\top(v-w)\quad\text{subject to}\quad
 \begin{cases}
 v,w\geq0,\quad\boldsymbol1^\top v=\boldsymbol1^\top w=1,
       \quad v_h,w_h\leq\alpha,\\
 l\leq t\leq u,\quad |P_kv-t|\leq\delta,
       \quad |P_kw-t|\leq\delta,\\
 |P_jv-P_jw|\leq2\delta.
 \end{cases}
\]
The last inequality is exactly the condition that the two possible second
answers have overlapping error intervals.  Thus every terminal pair of
compatible records is included.  Maximizing these bounds over branches and
contrasts certifies the frozen policy for every allowed record and error
vector.  Closed branch endpoints can overcover a tie but cannot invalidate
the upper bound.  A fixed batch uses the analogous two-prefix constraints,
without a branching variable; all ten batches are evaluated.

\paragraph{A bound against every randomized batch.}
A feasible endpoint pair $v,w$ for batch $Q$ with joint contrast difference
strictly exceeding $1/2$ supplies one adverse record: choose actual profile
$v$, set $e_q=(P_qw-P_qv)/2$ for $q\in Q$, and set the other errors to zero.
This complete latent record is fixed independently of the batch selected
later.  Compatible endpoint alternatives certify additional failures of
other batches at the same record.  Unverified entries are conservatively
counted as success.  For any rational distribution $\lambda$ on these
records, the maximum, over the ten batches, of the resulting expected
success upper bounds also bounds the worst-case success probability of
every randomized batch.  This requires neither vertex sufficiency nor a
claim of equality with the full-continuum optimum.

\paragraph{Numerical certification.}
The checked objectives use exact rational normalization of the original
saved binary64 contrast coefficients.  Floating-point LP multipliers are
converted to rationals, their signs are enforced, and any stationarity
residual is bounded over the finite variable box.  This gives rigorous
upper bounds without assuming exact floating-point dual feasibility.
Specifically, for a minimization LP $\min f^\top z$ with $Az\leq b$,
$Hz=h$ and $L\leq z\leq U$, any rational $\lambda\leq0$ and unrestricted
$\nu$ give the lower bound
\[
 \lambda^\top b+\nu^\top h+
 \sum_i\min\{r_iL_i,r_iU_i\},\qquad
 r=f-A^\top\lambda-H^\top\nu.
\]
Indeed $\lambda^\top Az\geq\lambda^\top b$ and each residual term is
bounded over its interval.  Negating this bound for
$f^\top z=-c_r^\top(v-w)$ yields the exported width upper certificate.
Rational feasible endpoints give lower bounds.  A separate solver-free
checker verifies source hashes, coefficients, every reported precision
status, all policy branches, and the fixed adverse mixtures.  These checks
certify the declared mathematical instances; they do not establish physical
sensor accuracy or field savings.
