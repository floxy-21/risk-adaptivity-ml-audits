\subsection{A certificate gate for proposed model comparisons}
\label{sec:practical_gate}
An imputation rule can propose a model ranking before its robustness to
missing verification targets has been established. We implement a separate
gate that receives that proposed direction and the two certificate endpoints,
and returns \emph{allow}, \emph{hold}, or \emph{contradict}. A proposal is allowed
only when the entire interval supports its direction, using the existing
scaled $10^{-9}$ sign margin. The gate accepts no hidden reference targets.
Its output supports retrospective model-comparison decisions.

On the original Portugal archive-aware exact-total grid, paired certificates
allow 7,836 of 9,720 historical-donor proposals, compared with 2,513 for
separate-loss certificates under identical information and zero additional
queries. The number held falls from 7,207 to 1,884, a derived reduction of
73.86\% in unresolved certificates. All 1,884 held paired comparisons have a
sharp endpoint supporting the opposite ranking beyond the numerical decision
margin. Consequently, the declared information admits an opposing completion
even though the donor's realized ranking is correct in every case. Neither
certificate method contradicts a donor proposal in this grid. This result
quantifies the distinction between an observed correct imputation and an
all-completions certificate. Separate-loss intervals are the matched
certificate comparator, not the strongest point-imputation predictor.

The executable action ledger covers all 20,088 public exact-total comparisons
across both access regimes and both public cohorts. It fixes gate decisions
before consulting reference contrasts. Constructing 40,176 gate outputs and
their ledger columns took 0.462\,s after CSV parsing; the complete replay took
7.704\,s. These are postprocessing times for frozen endpoints, not times for
regenerating the forecast archive or computing all bounds.

\subsection{Extended archive certification on the additional consumers}
\label{sec:extended_archive_replication}
To extend the earlier primary-scenario replication, we ran the full declared
comparison grid on the existing 30 additional Portugal consumers: three gap
lengths, three mask rules, three seed labels, two aggregate lengths, and all
six model pairs, separately for exact totals and 5\% total tolerance.
Each period comparison retains all 107 eligible days and a 2,568-target loss
denominator. The grid protocol and input hashes were saved before this run;
no models were refitted or cases removed by outcome. These consumers had
already contributed to an earlier six-hour recovery analysis. This is a new
full-grid computation from frozen forecasts, not a newly untouched source
or a blind prospective replication. The deterministic high-demand masks use
the serialized targets with stable tie breaking; repeated seed labels for
that rule are dependent stress cases.

The archive reconstruction uses only saved persistence prediction rows and
their documented one-day copy relationship. Exact-total singleton and
zero-residual deductions precede both bound methods. Every permitted direct
copy is used before uncertainty is calculated. Of 3,210 meter-days, 3,060
are completely reconstructed and therefore yield point intervals for every
grid setting; the implementation bypasses unnecessary optimizations in
those cases. The 346,680 day--scenario evaluations require 16,200 bound
calls for the remaining days. The fresh computation and output generation
took 39.35\,s. Neither bound method issues an incorrect comparison against
the held reference, and every separate-loss certificate is also paired-
certified. The comparison counts are dependent, so they are not interpreted
as independent Bernoulli trials.

An independently assembled whole-period linear program checks 432 cases
covering the first and last declared consumers, every gap, mask, aggregate
length and tolerance, and the first seed. Its 864 endpoint optimizations
agree with the production paired endpoints to $1.14\times10^{-13}$ in
absolute units and $1.67\times10^{-15}$ after the declared endpoint scaling;
the largest LP constraint violation is zero at the stored precision.
The LP feasibility tolerances are $10^{-9}$, and the endpoint comparison
tolerance is $10^{-8}\max(1,|D^-|,|D^+|)$. This checker takes 19.86\,s and
imports no production bound routine. It checks the paired-bound evaluation,
not an independent forecast refit or every separate-loss quadratic program.
These checks validate the extended grid's paired-certificate calculations.
