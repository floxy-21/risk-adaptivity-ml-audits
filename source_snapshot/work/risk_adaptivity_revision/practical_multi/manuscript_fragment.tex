\subsection{Authenticating a selected model against all alternatives}
\label{sec:practical-model-selection}
A practical certificate gate can accompany an existing model-selection
rule. First, the historical donor imputation proposes the model with the
smallest completed-period MSE among persistence, ridge, gradient boosting
and the MLP. Second, the gate authenticates that proposal only if its
paired interval certifies a lower MSE than each of the other three models.
Otherwise it returns unresolved. Selection uses the saved donor loss
differences alone; hidden targets are inspected only after selection and
certification. A unique donor preference is required, and nonunique cases
would remain in the denominator. Both bound methods receive the same
proposed model and the same archive information, with zero additional
queries.

This secondary analysis aggregates the existing six pair comparisons into
one four-model selection scenario; it is not an independent replication.
For the Portugal exact-total archive, paired bounds authenticate 933 of
1,620 proposals (57.59\%), compared with 167 (10.31\%) using separate-loss
bounds. The difference is 47.28 percentage points, with a descriptive
whole-consumer bootstrap interval of 40.80--53.77 points. Unresolved
proposals decrease from 1,453 to 687, a 52.72\% reduction relative to the
separate-bound unresolved count. This is a reduction in unresolved
benchmark cases, not measured staff time, retrieval cost or deployment
failures. The proposed models are gradient boosting in 1,242 scenarios,
the MLP in 216 and ridge in 162; these repeated scenarios share 30 consumers.

All scenarios have a unique donor preference, and none has a contrary
certificate or an erroneous proposed winner relative to the reference
period. Thus the result demonstrates additional all-completions
authentication, not an accuracy improvement over the donor. It also does
not establish that a certified historical winner will outperform the
other models on future data. Among the 933 authenticated Portugal
proposals, the median guaranteed minimum MSE advantage over the three
competitors is $0.001382$ times the squared training-period mean target;
this scale is fixed from training data and is not a percentage reduction
in unknown test MSE.

The complete public replay checks 5,022 Portugal/household scenarios over
the three tolerances; the local full-source check adds 486 counter
scenarios. The source exports donor proposals only for exact totals, so
the tolerance analysis explicitly holds that proposal fixed and does not
claim a fresh donor imputation at larger tolerances. The checker verifies
all six source pair rows, additive donor scores, interval nesting, sign
rules, the three required favorable comparisons and monotonicity under
widening totals. The supplement includes the case ledger, input hashes
and reproduction command. Bootstrap intervals resample all 54 scenarios
of a consumer together in 2,000 draws. The reported 95\% interval is the
percentile interval, using the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the bootstrap
mean coverage difference with NumPy's default linear quantile interpolation.
The generator is \texttt{numpy.random.default\_rng(20261012)}.
It is reinitialized for each cohort/tolerance group, so the same consumer
index draws are reused across Portugal tolerances.
The intervals describe convenience-panel
stability and do not capture shared calendar shocks. The one-household
and three-counter cohorts are not assigned population confidence intervals.
