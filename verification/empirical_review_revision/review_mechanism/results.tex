\paragraph{Which cases isolate sharing, and which depend on absolute precision?}
Of the 225 two-versus-three reading separations, 191 are joint-only:
137 original and 54 external blocks have scalar minimum fixed budgets of at
most two for every contrast. Within these 191 blocks, two contrasts suffice
to cause the obstruction in 168, while 23 require at least three. The other
34 blocks contain a scalar contrast that already needs three fixed readings.
Among the 222 matched-half certificates, 188 are joint-only.

The absolute-floor calculation at the primary setting identifies 186 of the 225 baseline
reading-saving blocks where feedback remains necessary: all 168 original
blocks and 18 external blocks. The other 39 external blocks meet the
requirement with a fixed budget of zero (15 blocks), one (14), or two (10)
readings. Two further external blocks become
two-versus-three separations, giving 20 across the complete external panel.
The all-block fixed/adaptive-fallback totals are 2625/2457 original and
378/358 external, or 6.40\% and 5.29\% fewer readings, respectively.
The floor-aware planner certifies the unchanged 57-block external subset
with 70 readings, compared with the minimum fixed total of 88 (20.45\%
fewer). Its exported plans specify all fixed timestamps and adaptive
branches; the verifier checks every block's guarantee directly.

Initial loss-scale widths explain the different responses. Among the
individually binding contrasts in the 225 blocks, the original median
$W_{r,0}$ is 0.12536 squared kWh (interquartile range 0.07424--0.27658);
the external median is 0.001905 (0.000741--0.008365).
There are 136 and 47 such contrasts, respectively; 69 blocks have no single
deletion-binding contrast because of redundant constraints.
