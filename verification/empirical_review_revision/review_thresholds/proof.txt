THRESHOLD-INTERVAL CERTIFICATE

Let F={x>=0:sum_h x_h=1}. For fixed prefix batch Q and normalized contrast r,
write its normalized interval width as v_Qr.x, where v_Qr,h is the range of
contrast r's coefficients in the segment of Q containing hour h. Thus
J_Q(x)=max_r v_Qr.x. All inputs below are exact rational numbers.

1. Frozen adaptive policy
For each stored branch t in [a,b], the first query is k, the second is j, and
sum_{h<=k} x_h=t. For a fixed contrast, the maximum width is the largest
coefficient v_Qr,h in the first region times t plus the largest coefficient
in the second region times (1-t). The maximum over contrasts is convex
piecewise affine in t, so its maximum on [a,b] is attained at an endpoint.
Check contiguous coverage of [0,1], evaluate both endpoints of every branch,
and let A be the largest value. Then the frozen adaptive policy guarantees
J<=A for every x in F. Endpoint ties are harmless because both adjacent
branch choices are checked at their common endpoint.

2. Frozen uniform mixture of two batches Q1,Q2
For every contrast pair r,s, set
  p_rs = min_{lambda in [0,1]} max_h {
                 lambda v_Q1r,h + (1-lambda) v_Q2s,h }.
This is computed exactly at endpoints or pairwise intersections of the six
affine functions of lambda. A multiplier attaining p_rs certifies that both
v_Q1r.x and v_Q2s.x cannot exceed p_rs simultaneously. Set P=max_rs p_rs.
If both J_Q1(x)>P and J_Q2(x)>P, choose their maximizing r,s, contradicting
the multiplier inequality. Therefore at least one selected batch succeeds
at every x whenever tau>=P. Their fixed equal mixture has success >=1/2.

The independent checker derives p_rs from the primal problem
  max_{x in F} min(v_Q1r.x,v_Q2s.x).
A hypograph vertex projects either to a simplex vertex or to a two-coordinate
edge point where the two forms agree. Enumerating these points produces a
matching rational value without using the producer's dual calculation.

3. Frozen adverse distribution
Let mu be the stored probability distribution over feasible adverse records.
For each of the ten two-prefix batches Q define
  U_Q = min { z in {J_Q(v):mu_v>0} :
                        sum_v mu_v 1{J_Q(v)<=z} > 1/2 }.
Such a value exists because eventually every record succeeds. Put U=min_Q U_Q.
For every tau<U and every fixed batch Q, its success averaged over mu is at
most 1/2. The same holds for every randomized mixture of fixed batches.
Consequently some feasible record has success at most 1/2 for that mixture,
giving an upper bound of 1/2 on the full continuum minimax success. A finite
adverse set is sufficient for this upper bound. Fewer than two readings cannot
improve this value: adding a prefix observation only narrows all intervals,
so every smaller batch is dominated by one of the enumerated two-query batches.

4. Matching bounds
Set L=max(A,P). For every tau in [L,U), adaptive success is 1, the uniform
fixed mixture gives a lower guarantee 1/2, and the adverse distribution gives
the upper bound 1/2. Thus the optimal randomized batch success is exactly 1/2.
Success uses the closed event J<=tau, which makes U an excluded endpoint.
The complete upper-endpoint behavior is retained as each batch's success
strictly below and at its first crossover.

Scope
The interval is sufficient for these frozen policies and adverse weights.
The method does not decide what happens outside it after choosing different
policies or adverse distributions. The calculation is an exact-total,
uncapped, noiseless guarantee; physical capacity and error models are separate.
No recorded energy allocation is used to select the threshold or the policy.
