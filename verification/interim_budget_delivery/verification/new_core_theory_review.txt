CORE PROOF CHECKS
1. Fixed-set sharp width is finite, nonnegative, concave, and upper semicontinuous on compact convex F. Convex combinations of endpoint witnesses prove concavity; convergent subsequences of endpoint witnesses prove upper semicontinuity even when F has boundary degeneracy.
2. The minimax interchange uses the unpenalized mean-width payoff. It is continuous/affine in the finite batch mixture, and concave/upper semicontinuous in the record. Compactness and continuity then attain both optima. Sion's Theorem 3.4 has the stated hypotheses. No continuity of the penalty is inserted implicitly into this interchange.
3. The hardest record bounds every admissible final set at once. With the internal seed fixed, identical queried answers reproduce each next query and the stopping decision. Therefore the final information fiber equals the declared final-set fiber. Randomness is independent of the hidden record; admissibility is answer independent. These assumptions are all stated. The proof does not claim validity for an adversary that changes the latent noise after each query.
4. Finite-mixture Jensen is sufficient for any finite-valued nondecreasing concave penalty, including a downward endpoint jump at zero. The printed indicator 1{w>0} is indeed concave on [0,infinity). The argument does not require a continuous extension to zero.
5. Necessity handles all monotone nonconcave penalties: midpoint concavity plus monotonicity implies full concavity using dyadic coefficients approaching the desired coefficient from below. The limit is taken on the finite affine combination of values, not on phi. The three-coordinate polytope realizes the strict midpoint violation. At (1/2,0,0), all final batches retain at least (l+h)/2; the endpoint adversarial mixture supplies the strict batch lower bound. Fewer queries cannot escape the lower bound.
6. The K-family precision upper bound correctly uses interior capacity records, not only concentration vertices. This avoids the common error of maximizing threshold indicators at vertices without justification.
7. The enumerative hardest-record LP has independent endpoint witnesses for each legal batch and a common feasible record. The extracted multipliers sum to one. The two-query LP correctly shares the first answer while giving each second action separate endpoints. Measurability and rational-answer bit complexity are explicitly handled.
8. The Helly consequence is a scalar statement. Its strict failure sets are convex; finite Helly does not require their closure. It is already labelled adapted from sparse-policy bounds. The joint comparison does not incorrectly apply the scalar concavity theorem to a maximum of widths.
9. The shared construction proves both optima, not only achievable values. I independently evaluated its printed coefficients using Python Fraction arithmetic (no existing optimizer/checker import). The hardest-record array was [1,3/4,5/6,1/2,1/2,5/6,1/2,3/4,1/2,1/2]. The adverse expectations were [2/3,2/3,2/3,2/3,2/3,13/18,5/6,5/6,2/3,2/3]. The proposed batch mixture at all six vertices gave [2/3,2/3,2/3,2/3,2/3,1/2]. Thus the printed lower/upper arrays agree exactly. The adaptive branches have worst-width envelopes max(t,1/3) and max(1/2,1-t); choosing at t=1/2 proves the 1/2 upper guarantee. Convexity of each joint batch width justifies the vertex upper bound for mean risk, but not for threshold success.
10. The exact-simplex shared planner considers endpoints and every intersection of underlying affine lines, including across different second actions. Between such breakpoints the max/min envelope selects one line. This is sufficient. Floating-point shortcuts are disclosed separately from exact rational guarantee certificates.
