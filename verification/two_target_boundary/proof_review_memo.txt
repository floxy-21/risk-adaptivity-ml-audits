DECISIVE FINDING
The proposed construction is correct for every integer m>=3. The lower
guarantees hold on the entire compact convex polytope, not a finite grid.
The finite adverse records suffice for the matching full-continuum upper
bound. This yields adaptive success one and optimal randomized-batch
success exactly 1/m under the same two-coordinate hard budget.

EXHAUSTIVE QUERY CHECK
There are seven unordered types of distinct two-query batches:
{t,z_j}, {t,u}, {t,v}, {u,v}, {u,z_j}, {v,z_j}, {z_j,z_k}.
At x_i=((i-1/2)/m,0,0,0,...,0), only {t,z_j} can succeed; its maximum
width is H+|i-j|-3/4, so success occurs exactly when j=i.
The candidate theorem includes all seven exact width pairs and elementary
inequalities for the failing types. The earlier informal message's 'six
types' meant the six alternatives to {t,z_j}; the full table has seven.
Shorter/repeated/stopped executions are covered by monotonicity of sharp
intervals under extending their queried sets to two distinct coordinates.

MATCHING LOWER BOUNDS
Query t, then z_j for (j-1)/m<=t<=j/m. Endpoint ties use smallest j;
t=0 and t=1 are covered. Each width is at most H-1/4, independently
of the observed z_j. Uniformly choosing {t,z_j} before observing anything
has success at least 1/m at every record, because these branches cover
[0,1]. Uniform adverse mass over x_i makes every batch's average success
at most 1/m; mixture averaging and min<=average give the robust upper bound.
The adverse record distribution is fixed before the random seed.

SCALAR AND THREE-QUERY CHECKS
Each scalar already has a deterministic fixed two-query guarantee:
{t,z_m} gives W1<=H-1/4; {t,z_1} gives W2<=H-1/4.
The simpler {t,u} and {t,v} batches also work at half-width, with width H.
The three-query batch {t,z_1,z_m} gives both first widths <=H-3/2.
Therefore three fixed queries, versus two adaptive queries, suffice; this
family does not establish an unbounded query-count or cost reduction.

ADDITIONAL VERIFIED STRENGTHENING: ALL THREE MODEL PAIRS
For T3=T1-T2, let P=floor(m^2/4). Its initial range is exactly
[-(H+P),H+P], and after t alone its sharp width is exactly m+2P.
This already meets the half-initial-width target. The common nonnegative
forecast realization in main_theorem.tex produces T1 and T2 as paired
MSE contrasts up to constants and T3 as the remaining model pair.
Consequently the exact separation holds for all three pairwise comparisons
among three models, not only two comparisons selected against a baseline.
This does not mean that these constructed forecasts were fitted or useful
predictors on an observed application; the result is algebraic realizability.

THRESHOLD INTERVAL CHECK
For common normalized tau in [1/2-1/(8H),1/2+1/(8H)), the same all-three
claim holds. For T1,T2 this is absolute q in [H-1/4,H+1/4). Off-diagonal
{t,z_j} batches fail at width >=H+1/4; all six other types have larger
failing widths. At the upper endpoint adjacent batches can succeed, so the
open upper endpoint is necessary for this particular upper-bound argument.
The third target already meets the lower endpoint: the difference between
its allowed width there and m+2P is M-P-(1+P/H)/4>0 for m>=3.
Each scalar's fixed policy and the joint three-query policy above remain
valid throughout this interval. The normalized margin equals
1/[2m(2m+3)] and vanishes with m. No fixed positive measurement-error
tolerance is established by this calculation.

RELATION TO THE CURRENT SCALAR HALF-FLOOR
Current Theorem 3 assumes ONE affine target, a compact convex uncertainty
set, an exact first-stage linear observation with answer dimension r, and
a fixed finite answer-independent eligible continuation dictionary under
the same hard budget. Its conditional worst scalar width is concave; its
strict failure sets are convex and Helly gives at most r+1 actions.
This construction satisfies the feasible-set and query assumptions but
changes the objective to a maximum of two normalized widths. Its joint
conditional function is convex piecewise affine, not scalar-concave.
The good set for action z_j is an interval, while its strict failure set
can be the union of two separated intervals. The scalar theorem therefore
does not apply. No contradiction or error in that theorem was identified.
